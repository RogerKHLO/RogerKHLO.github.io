### The Problem
I got tired of paying for expensive, name-brand power banks that bloated and failed right after their warranties expired. From my own experience, the brands put marketing hype ahead of longevity: they cram in extreme charging speeds and seal everything in an airtight, un-repairable shell that slowly bakes the battery. So I engineered my own alternative that sidesteps that planned obsolescence.

### The Design
- **Optimised C-rate:** capped at 22.5W (about 0.6C) instead of the aggressive 1.5C to 2C retail rates, which minimises internal chemical friction and heat.
- **Conservative BMS profile:** an Ismartware (智融) **SW6201** board (T2410 v1.0) enforces a strict 4.2V cutoff to protect the **1260110 10000mAh LiPo** cell from premature degradation.
- **Thermal isolation:** a **PLA 3D-printed** enclosure with scrap foam insulation keeps hot board components away from the cell, and passive air gaps channel heat out.
- **Mechanical flex space:** even internal clearance lets the cell expand slightly without creating pressure hot-spots.
- **True serviceability:** no anti-repair glue. The chassis is held together with screws, and the modular layout allows easy battery swaps and resoldering. The band around the case is an aesthetic choice only; the case is secured by the screws.
- **Minimalist UI:** the viewing window over the **TFT display** is just an offcut of scrap acrylic I had lying around, and it exposes only the key battery information.
